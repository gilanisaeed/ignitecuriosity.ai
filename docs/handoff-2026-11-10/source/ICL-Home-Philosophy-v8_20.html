Our missionIgnite Curiosity Lab creates AI learning experiences that build independent reasoning skills in K-12 students, where AI guides their thinking instead of giving them answers.

As AI makes answers effortless, we design learning in which the student does the reasoning: forms the idea, tests it, revises it and explains it. One question guides everything we build: What thinking must remain with the learner?

From prompts to pedagogy

We make pedagogy part of the architecture.

A common approach in educational AI is to ask the model to follow pedagogical instructions. We go further: when a learning principle matters, we design the system around it.

The default

Prompt-level guidance

The model is instructed to follow a pedagogical rule.
Reliability depends on model compliance and prompt robustness.
Behavior can drift across prompts, models, and model updates.
What we build

System-level learning design

Critical constraints are enforced by the system, not requested of the model.
Any model we use works inside explicit system boundaries, and it is never given the answer.
Those constraints are checked directly: we test for answer leakage instead of assuming it away.

When pedagogy is critical, we prefer enforceable constraints over model promises.

The question we ask

Instead of asking what AI can do for the learner, we ask what thinking must remain with the learner.

System
Learner

AI can support

  • Ask a question
  • Surface a pattern
  • Offer a scaffold
  • Prompt reflection

The learner must own

  • Form the hypothesis
  • Choose the approach
  • Make the mistake
  • Explain the reasoning

Three findings, three design decisions

01

Struggle is only productive by design

Finding

Problem solving before instruction can deepen understanding and transfer, but the effect depends on age, task, guidance, and implementation. In the meta-analysis, learners in grades 2 to 5 did better with instruction first.

Kapur 2008, 2016 · Sinha & Kapur 2021, meta-analysis
Design decision

We protect the learner’s effort without assuming struggle itself teaches. We scaffold support and design each struggle to resolve in sense-making.

02

Assisted performance is not enough

Finding

About 1,000 high-school math students in Turkey, randomized. With unrestricted GPT-4, practice performance improved, but later unaided exam performance was 17 percent lower than the no-AI control. A guided version that gave hints instead of answers showed no significant reduction.

Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS
Design decision

We record the help behind every attempt, so an assisted score is never mistaken for what a learner can do alone. And the AI is never given the answer, so the restraint does not rest on instructions to the model.

03

Support can create dependence

Finding

1,625 university students across ten courses, randomized. For four weeks, AI prompts flagged weak comments in their peer feedback, without writing it. When the prompts were withdrawn, their comments were flagged as low quality significantly more often. The researchers concluded that students tended to rely on the assistance rather than learn from it.

Darvishi et al. 2024, Computers & Education
Design decision

Support should work itself out of a job. Falling hint use counts as progress only if the learner still solves without it.

What does success look like?

The learner needs us less.

Most software measures success by how often people come back. We ask a harder question: does the learner need less help as they go, and still solve without it?

Human-led, AI-supported

Human judgment stays central. Teachers direct the tools they use in class, and in learner-facing experiences the learner owns the reasoning. AI supports both. It replaces neither.

Explainable by design

Every AI intervention has a pedagogical purpose we can name. Where the system steps in, we design that step to be visible to the teacher or parent, not hidden inside the experience.

Privacy is part of the architecture

We minimize the data we collect about a learner, define clear retention boundaries, never sell or rent it, and design for age-appropriate privacy from the start. Our privacy approach → blocker: page must exist before launch
