About Ignite Curiosity Lab

We build AI that leaves the thinking with the learner.

Our missionIgnite Curiosity Lab creates AI learning experiences that build independent reasoning skills in K-12 students, where AI guides their thinking instead of giving them answers.

We are educators, researchers and engineers. We start with how children learn to reason, then ask what technology can add. Never the other way around.

Why we started

Answers are nearly free now. The thinking still has to be the child’s.

AI can make knowledge more accessible than at any moment in history. It can also make it remarkably easy to stop thinking. For a child, the shortcut is one tap away, and AI can do children’s thinking for them.

optional: the moment that started Ignite, in one true sentence

So the skill that matters is no longer knowing the answer. It is knowing what to ask, how to test an idea, and how to think alongside intelligent machines without handing over your own mind.

We started Ignite Curiosity Lab to design for that. The order matters: first a theory of how children learn to reason and what we want to strengthen in them, then the question of what technology can add.

And we build for the student without a tutor at home. Learning technology that reaches only the children who already have every advantage is incomplete. sign-off: belief 04, moved here from home on 9 Sep

A learning company that happens to use AI. Not an AI company that happens to sell to schools.

Where we’re going

IgniteThinking is where we start.

Subject matter is the vehicle. Independent reasoning is the point. Each step widens the grades and subjects, and one gate decides when we take it.

  1. Now

    IgniteThinking

    Grades 4 and 5. Computational thinking puzzles and math puzzles, worked through a design thinking process.

  2. Next

    Full grade 4 and 5 math

    The same grades and subject, as the puzzle catalog completes.

  3. Evidence gate

    Measured first

    A controlled study shows a gain in independent reasoning.

  4. Later

    Across K-12

    New grade bands and subjects, each with its own validation.

At every step, the AI guides the thinking and is never given the answer.

A review commissioned by UNESCO reaches the same rule we hold: evidence that a system benefits one age group does not necessarily transfer to another (Livingstone et al., 2026).
