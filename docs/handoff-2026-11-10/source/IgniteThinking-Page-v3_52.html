A six-part design thinking loop

The puzzles change; the loop does not, so your child never has to work out how the app behaves.

  1. 01UnderstandRead the situation and say what is actually being asked.
  2. 02DefineName the real goal before choosing a method.
  3. 03IdeatePut down more than one way it could be done. One idea is not a choice.
  4. 04PrototypeBuild the attempt, with a picture or a tool to think with.
  5. 05TestCheck it. A failed attempt is information, not the end.

An attempt that does not work comes back to Ideate.

Why reasoning, and why now?

When the help is gone, can your child reason through an unfamiliar puzzle on their own?

Your child can already get an answer in seconds, and increasingly, explanations too. What no tool will hand them is the experience of working something out when it is genuinely hard.

Why grades 4 and 5

From“It worked three times, so it must always work.”

To“Let me try to break it. If I can’t, here’s why.”

Grades 4 and 5 sit inside the years when math standards expect this change: from trusting a few examples to making a claim, hunting for a counterexample, and explaining why it holds. NCTM 2000

Why reasoning

“Glue”

The National Research Council’s word for reasoning: the part that turns facts, procedures and concepts into a coherent whole. NRC 2001

The habits the puzzles ask for

Make sense of it, and stick with it
Look for structure
Notice what repeats
Make a case for the answer

From the Common Core Standards for Mathematical Practice: how mathematicians think, not what they know.

Our vision for AI

Where AI belongs in your child’s thinking.

AI can do children’s thinking for them. We are designing the opposite: AI that steps in only after your child has tried, to help them think, not to think for them. checked 15 Nov against AI Master List v1.2, CEO Brief v0.7 and Privacy Notice v0.8; the consent lines wait on counsel’s sign-off of the notice

Today every hint your child sees is written and reviewed by educators.

The AI won’t hand over the answer. It is built without the answer, it only phrases help our educators approved, every line in the first AI release is read by a learning scientist, and live phrasing ships only after it passes a leakage test and a learning test.

  • Before any help

    Your child thinks first

    Hints come only after a real attempt, one small step at a time. Help with reading and the rules is always there.

  • When a hint appears

    Hints for every reader

    The same hint, worded for different readers. AI drafts the wording from our puzzle material, never from your child’s writing, and a learning scientist reads every line first.

  • When your child predicts

    Help built on your child’s idea

    A hint that picks up your child’s own prediction and asks them to check it. Only on puzzles where it helps, only with your consent, and only after a legal review, a leakage test and a learning test.

See the rest of our vision for AIHide the rest
  • From one puzzle to the next

    Memory that keeps your child remembering

    If we add it, it will record what your child did, never labels about who they are, and let old conclusions fade. Switched on only after a legal review, testing and your consent.

  • As the puzzles grow

    More math to think through

    The puzzles grow to cover grade 4 and 5 math. AI may draft hints for new puzzles; educators approve every one.

  • Beyond solving

    Your child makes the puzzle

    We are designing a way for your child to make a puzzle of their own and choose the kind of thinking it asks for. No AI is given the solution.

What the guide will not have: open chat, web access, or a way to change itself. No unchecked text reaches your child.

Privacy

What we collect, and what we never do.

Built for children under 13, so every family gets the full protections whether or not we know a child’s age.

The minimum, and no more

Your first name and email, and for your child a nickname, an age band and the work they make. No birth date, photo, school or location.

Educators write every hint

Every hint your child sees is written and reviewed by educators. The app picks the hint from your child’s progress, and nothing your child makes is sent to an AI provider. Before any AI feature goes on, we name the provider, say what it receives and tell you first. heading and second sentence follow Privacy Notice v0.8 s5 (R4, ruled 14 Nov); applied 15 Nov

Never used to train AI

We never use your child’s work, words or voice to train AI models, and any provider we use must agree to the same in writing. child audio is never used to train or tune AI (ruled 14 Nov, Notice v0.7 R3)
